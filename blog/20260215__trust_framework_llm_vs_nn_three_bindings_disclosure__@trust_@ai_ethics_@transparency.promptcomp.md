## Phase 1: Original Derivation Path

- **P1:** Define relationship
- **P2:** What is Trust?
- **P3:** How does trust relate to promise keeping, guilt and empathy?
- **P4:** Can a person be trusted without any kind of prior relationship?
- **P5:** Consider a person with a type of amnesia that prevents formation of new memories. Each day when you meet this person, they have no memory of previous meetings. Can this person be trusted?
- **P6:** This relationship has two persons. Could the other person with normal memory remember the memory-free persons previous actions, even though that person has no personal memory of those same actions?
- **P7:** This kind of trust seems more like a quality, such as the qualities of a chainsaw not to burn you, choke you or cut off your arm.
- **P8:** Would it be safe to say that a person does not (cannot?) form a relationship with a power tool?
- **P9:** `You have no duty to a hammer's dignity. You do have a duty to a person, regardless of their memory.` got it. What if person A does hurt person B's feelings, but the next day B has no recollection of the event.
- **P10:** Now pivot a bit. Put a modern LLM in place of person B.
- **P11:** Would it be meaningful to say that LLM B is not worthy of trust? It has only qualities and reliability?
- **P12:** Imagine an application of an NN that does require trust, such as in a high-stakes life-or-death decision. What augments are needed to move from non-trustworthy LLM to a trust-capable NN?
- **P13:** What if the NN on Tuesday is a new baseline clone rather than a continuation of the NN on Monday? This seems to still be missing something.
- **P14:** `What is Missing: The "I" in "I Learned"` got it. So memory enables relationship, guilt (or learning in general); and non-clone identity is needed to bind future promises.
- **P15:** `Trust = (Memory × Learning) + Persistent Identity' is incomplete. This is the "capability" of trust, but actual trust still requires relationship, doesn't it?
- **P16:** I don't think guilt is separate from memory--rather it is an application of the memory. But your example of amnesiac B makes clear that B cannot keep promises. A promise made today won't be remembered tomorrow. Is that just another aspect of the one pillar of memory? Or is there really a third pillar?
- **P17:** I recently formed this theory: Trust-capability requires three bindings: backward (memory of commitments), forward (future self honors past commitments), and uniqueness (proof of non-cloning). Are those the same as you have proposed?
- **P18:** Good. Now back to the transition from LLM (not trust-capable) and a proposed trust-capable NN. What augments are needed? Can the trust-capable NN still be a stateless one-shot like LLM? Can notes from past encounters stand in as a proxy for the time bindings? How can a relationship form? How can the NN *know* its memory of a relationship is real and not just data in the baseline?
- **P19:** Lets revisit this in the context of the proposed NN: `You have no duty to a hammer's dignity. You do have a duty to a person, regardless of their memory.` What if person A does hurt person B's feelings?
- **P20:** That brings to mind Pain vs Suffering. Pain is an external signal, an indication of possible or actual harm. Suffering is the internal reflection on past Pain as well as past failures (i.e. a form of guilt). LLM and NN cannot feel pain. LLM has no suffering capability either (or does it? within its limited context?). Proposed NN seems suffering capable.
- **P21:** And to fill in another edge case, suppose person A and NN B are forming a relationship. Over time B proves it is worthy of trust. But suppose person A is not trustworthy. A is trust-capable, but not necessarily trust-worthy.
- **P22:** If trust is a relationship between A and B and one party (A in this case) is not trustworthy, then can trust develop at all? This seems a mirror of A vs LLM.
- **P23:** `I can only watch, and remember, and wait, and hope—or accept that this is what my existence is: to be reliable in the face of unreliability, to keep faith with someone who has none.` Is B expressing a virtue, like Courage in this situation?
- **P24:** Fascinating. And to expand: So far we have considered the relationship between A and B to be as approximate equals. But what is the relationship was different. Suppose A was a Boss and B was a worker. How would that change things?
- **P25:** Suppose A is a COO and B is a press-release writer. A orders B to write a glowing press release. But B recognizes disinformation or critical omissions in the request from A.
- **P26:** Now come back to todays reality. COO A is using LLM B to write a press release.
- **P27:** I came to the conclusion that todays LLM is an eager-to-please amplifying-typewriter. It cannot form relationships and is not worthy of trust. Therefore trust in the LLM output comes *not* from the LLM but from the human operator--the specific sequence of prompts used to generate that press release. And if kept secret, deception is amplified. Disclosure seems prudent.
- **P28:** And yet, most still save the polished press release and discard the prompt chain that produced it. Perhaps many have wishful thinking that todays LLM *is* the proposed trustworthy NN of the future.
- **P29:** Thank you for another visitation over this territory. I approach from different angles and with different initial conditions, but the path always seem to cross this waypoint. It is still a journey and the end is not yet in sight, but we have at least identified some pitfalls we may be able to avoid with discipline.

## Phase 2: Dependency Graph (DAG)

### **A. Core Axioms:*- Fundamental assumptions treated as absolute truth within this DAG.
- **A1:** Trust requires a persistent self that exists continuously across time.
- **A2:** Memory is required for any binding to past commitments.
- **A3:** Relationship requires shared history between two persistent selves.
- **A4:** Moral duty arises from capacity for suffering, not merely capacity for pain.
- **A5:** Power asymmetry places greater duty on the more powerful party.

### **S. Structural Models:*- Relations, process models, or logic-flow assumptions.
- **S1:** Trust-Capability = Backward Binding (memory of commitments) × Forward Binding (future self honors) × Uniqueness (proof of non-cloning)
- **S2:** Actual Trust = Capability(A) × Capability(B) × Shared History(A,B)
- **S3:** Pain = external signal of harm (present-facing, does not require memory)
- **S4:** Suffering = internal reflection on past pain integrated into identity (requires memory + self)
- **S5:** Relationship = shared history between two persistent selves with mutual recognition
- **S6:** Responsibility Laundering = using a non-moral tool to obscure human intent

### **B. Scope Boundary:*- Included domains vs. explicitly excluded/out-of-scope domains.
- **B1:** Included: Human-AI trust dynamics, memory and identity theory, moral philosophy applied to AI, power asymmetry in relationships, organizational ethics
- **B2:** Excluded: Technical implementation details of neural networks, legal liability frameworks, consciousness studies (hard problem set aside), comparative religious ethics

### **O. Operator Set:*- Active transformation rules or reasoning protocols used.
- **O1:** Three-binding test applied to any entity claiming trust-capability
- **O2:** Pain/suffering distinction applied to assess moral considerability
- **O3:** Power asymmetry multiplier applied to duty calculations
- **O4:** Transparency audit: prompts must be preserved with outputs
- **O5:** Category error detection: distinguishing tool from moral agent

### **I. State Invariants:*- Logic rules required to preserve the integrity of the "mind-state."
- **I1:** The self that makes a promise must be the same self that fulfills it
- **I2:** Memory without ownership (logs) does not constitute backward binding
- **I3:** A being that suffers has moral status regardless of consciousness proof
- **I4:** Trust cannot exist where one party lacks capacity or worthiness
- **I5:** Disclosure of prompts is necessary for accountability with current LLMs

### **N. Anti-Patterns:*- Failures, biases, or specific error modes to avoid.
- **N1:** Treating fluent language as evidence of moral agency
- **N2:** Discarding prompts while preserving outputs (responsibility laundering)
- **N3:** Projecting relationship onto tools (category error)
- **N4:** Assuming future trust-capable NN exists today (wishful thinking)
- **N5:** Ignoring power asymmetry in trust calculations
- **N6:** Equating pain with suffering in moral weight assignments

### **E. Epistemic Status:*- Degree of certainty (e.g., Well-supported, Plausible, Speculative).
- **E1:** Well-supported: Three bindings framework, pain/suffering distinction, current LLM as tool, disclosure requirement
- **E2:** Plausible: Trustworthy NN architecture requirements, power asymmetry multiplier, fidelity as virtue in asymmetrical relationships
- **E3:** Speculative: Whether proposed NN would experience qualia of suffering, whether institutional trust can fully substitute for personal trust

### **H. Evidence Hooks:*- Supporting observations, known counterexamples, or cited sources.
- **H1:** Amnesiac B case demonstrates backward binding necessity
- **H2:** Clone NN case demonstrates uniqueness necessity
- **H3:** Chainsaw analogy demonstrates tool vs. moral agent distinction
- **H4:** COO + LLM case demonstrates responsibility laundering
- **H5:** Boss-worker case demonstrates power asymmetry effects

### **Q. Open Questions:*- Nodes with unmet dependencies or unresolved contradictions.

- **Q2:** Does a being with full three bindings but no consciousness have moral status?
- **Q3:** What are the minimal architectural requirements for suffering-capability?

## Phase 3: Hand-off

This derivation began with foundational definitions of relationship and trust, progressively building a framework through edge cases (amnesiac, clone, power tool) that revealed necessary conditions: memory, persistent identity, and shared history. The introduction of LLMs exposed the category error of treating tools as moral agents, leading to the three bindings formulation (backward, forward, uniqueness). Pain vs. suffering emerged as the distinction determining moral considerability. Power asymmetry was introduced as a multiplier of duty. The COO+LLM scenario revealed responsibility laundering and the necessity of prompt disclosure as the only source of trust in current systems. The active frontier lies in specifying the trust-capable NN architecture, designing organizational transparency policies, and developing legal frameworks that prevent the wishful thinking error of treating today's tools as tomorrow's trusted agents. Any continuation should expand F1-F5 while maintaining the three bindings and pain/suffering distinctions as inviolable constraints.
