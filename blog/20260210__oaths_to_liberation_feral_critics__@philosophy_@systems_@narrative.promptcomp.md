## Phase 2: Dependency Graph (DAG)

### A. Core Axioms
A1. An oath is a formal, binding promise that invokes a higher authority or value as a witness and guarantee of sincerity.
A2. The power of an oath derives from the consequences—internal (guilt, self-betrayal) and external (legal/social punishment)—of breaking it.
A3. Trust is the deeper anchor of an oath, formed through relationship, which requires time-bound continuity (memory + promise-keeping).
A4. The "Voting Integrity Trilemma" (Authorization, Anonymity, Verifiability) and the "Governance Trilemma" (Oath Mandate vs. Re-election Mandate vs. Tribal Loyalty) are irreducible constraint spaces.
A5. No system can perfectly enforce or verify internal state (sincerity, intent, fear).
A6. All governance systems structure incentives; they cannot eliminate the human dilemmas of power, tribalism, and hypocrisy.
A7. Symbolic power (dignified) and efficient power (governing) are distinct and can be separated or fused in a system's design.
A8. Dignity is the inherent, non-contingent worth of a being or office; it transforms power into authority and provides social legitimacy.
A9. The "feral hero" (Tarzan/Conan/Goku) represents a thought-experiment: an individual maturing free of cultural domestication, providing an outsider's critique of society.
A10. Domestication (Ruiz) is the process by which society installs its "Book of Law," creating an internal Judge and Victim. Liberation is the rewiring or rejection of these agreements.

### B. Operator Set
B1. Contrast internal trustworthiness (virtue ethics) with external enforcement (guardrails, consequences).
B2. Analyze any oath/promise through the lenses of consent, duress, and the nature of the enforcing power.
B3. Map systemic trust mechanisms from one domain (cryptographic voting, AI alignment) to another (political governance).
B4. Deconstruct a ritual (oath-taking) into its legal, symbolic, psychological, and political functions.
B5. Identify the selectorate: the specific group whose approval an official actually requires to gain/keep power.
B6. Compare governance systems by their structural incentives: majoritarian/winner-take-all vs. proportional/coalition-based.
B7. Separate the "double-body" of a fused Head of State/Head of Government to analyze stability, legitimacy, and tribal conflict.
B8. Apply the "Loki Test" (trickster stress-test) to any system to find gaps between symbol/reality, rule/incentive, word/intent, role/person.
B9. Analyze characters (Penguin, Vegeta, Mr. Satan, Goku) as embodied systemic critiques or models (kleptocracy, de-domestication, dignified shell, feral critic).
B10. Trace narrative lineage from archetype (Tarzan) to later iterations (Conan, Goku) to identify core philosophical propositions.
B11. Evaluate systems through the lens of Ruiz's Domestication: identify the "Judge," "Victim," and "Planet's Dream" at work.

### C. Anti-Patterns
C1. Relying solely on external guardrails/oaths without cultivating internal trustworthiness; this teaches deception.
C2. Systems where the selectorate is a narrower, more extreme group than the nominal constituency.
C3. Fusing symbolic national unity with partisan executive power, raising the stakes of political conflict to existential levels.
C4. Winner-take-all, geographically-based election systems in a mass-communication, national-identity era.
C5. Mistaking ritual performance for genuine commitment or effective constraint.
C6. Believing any system can resolve the fundamental tension between the universal ideal of governance and the particular demands of political survival.
C7. The "sincerity asymmetry" where the honest feel bound by oaths and the cynical treat them as tools, creating a selection advantage for hypocrites.
C8. "Oath inflation" or ritual saturation, which drains symbolic power through commodification.
C9. Designing systems that cannot model or defend against the trickster function, ensuring they will be optimized by tricksters.
C10. The "hypocrisy trap" becoming a "hypocrisy ladder" where strategic hypocrisy is the optimal survival algorithm.

### D. Structural & Execution Models
D1. The Oath as Multi-Layer Tool: Legal (redundant affirmation), Political (hypocrisy trap), Psychological (personal anchor), Social (legitimizing ritual).
D2. The Tribal Loyalty Engine: Survival depends on pleasing the selectorate (in-group), which rewards demonization of the out-group, conflicting with the universal oath mandate.
D3. Coalition Governance Model: Proportional representation forces pre-power negotiation (coalition agreement). Accountability via no-confidence votes trades stability for representativeness.
D4. Fused Executive Model (e.g., US Presidency): Symbolic and efficient power combined in one partisan office, elected via an anachronistic, distorting (Electoral College) system, intensifying the oath/tribe trap.
D5. Separated Executive Model: A symbolic, apolitical Head of State (dignified) provides continuity; a political Head of Government (efficient) is accountable to parliament. Lowers conflict stakes.
D6. The "Feral Critic" Model: A character raised outside domestication (Tarzan, Conan, Goku) uses first-principles reasoning and mimetic learning to expose societal absurdities and arbitrariness.
D7. The "Dignified Shell / Efficient Core" Model (Mr. Satan / Z-Fighters): A symbolic, possibly fraudulent, front (dignified) provides public stability and morale; the real power (efficient) operates hidden, protected by the shell.
D8. The Generational Courtesy Protocol: Rising power conceals its full scope from fading power to preserve dignity and stability, revealing it only in service to a transcendent cause.
D9. Domestication Architecture (Ruiz): Society installs the "Book of Law" -> creates internal Judge & Victim -> enforces the "Planet's Dream." Liberation requires breaking agreements and choosing new ones.

### E. State Invariants
E1. Maintain the distinction between an oath's *source of binding* (inherent in the office/role) and its *social power* (the performative ritual).
E2. Treat hypocrisy as a rational output of systems where selectorate incentives conflict with the nominal mandate, not merely a character flaw.
E3. Assume all real-world governance is a negotiation between the universal ideal (oath, common good) and the particular reality (tribal loyalty, re-election).
E4. Evaluate governance mechanisms by how they structure the "in-group/out-group" dynamic and the official's path to survival.
E5. Consider the "feral critic" as a lens for cognitive estrangement, revealing cultural assumptions by their absence.
E6. Recognize that fearlessness in the feral hero is not courage but a pre-cultural lack of threat perception, a different neurological condition.
E7. Understand narrative archetypes (Tarzan->Conan->Goku) as evolving thought-experiments on the same core question: the self after domestication.

F3. Under what conditions does a coalition-based system's tendency towards opaque, elite bargaining trigger a populist backlash that destabilizes it more severely than a majoritarian system?
F4. Is there a measurable "dignity threshold" of congruence between officeholder and office, below which the dignity of the office fails to attach?
F5. Can the "Generational Courtesy Protocol" be formalized into a system of succession that minimizes destabilizing conflict in non-familial hierarchies (e.g., corporate, academic)?
F6. What would a governance system explicitly designed to pass the "Loki Test" look like at scale? Can trickster energy be institutionally harnessed without being corrupted?
